BCS515B – Artificial Intelligence

Important Questions Based on VTU Model Paper, Dec 2024/Jan 2025 and June/July 2025 Papers

MODULE 1 – Introduction to AI and Intelligent Agents

Repeated 4 Times

  • Specify the PEAS description for a given intelligent agent/task environment.

Repeated 3 Times

  • Define Artificial Intelligence and explain its foundations / categories of AI.

Repeated 2 Times

  • Explain the characteristics of task environments, including observable/partially observable, single/multi-agent, deterministic/stochastic and static/dynamic environments.

Also Asked

  • Explain how agents interact with their environments through sensors and actuators.
  • Define Intelligence, AI, Agent, Rationality and Logical Reasoning.
  • Explain whether Artificial Intelligence is a science, engineering, or both.
  • Explain goal-based and utility-based agent programs with suitable pseudocode.

MODULE 2 – Problem-Solving Agents and Uninformed Search

Repeated 3 Times

  • Explain the components of a well-defined problem and formulate a problem using an example such as Vacuum World or the 8-puzzle.
  • Explain Breadth-First Search (BFS) and its evaluation criteria.
  • Explain Depth-Limited Search (DLS) and Iterative Deepening Depth-First Search (IDDFS).

Repeated 2 Times

  • Compare uninformed search strategies such as BFS, Uniform-Cost Search (UCS), DFS and DLS with respect to completeness, time complexity, space complexity and optimality.

Also Asked

  • Explain a simple problem-solving agent with its algorithm and assumptions.
  • Represent the state space for Vacuum World.
  • Define State Space, Search Node, Transition Model and Branching Factor with examples.
  • Formulate the 8-puzzle problem completely.
  • Prove or disprove that BFS is a special case of UCS.
  • Prove or disprove that UCS is a special case of A*.
  • Explain the reachability of states in the 8-puzzle and how to determine whether a state is reachable.

MODULE 3 – Informed Search and Knowledge-Based Agents

Repeated 5 Times

  • Apply A* Search to a given graph/problem, showing the stages of search and determining the solution path.

Repeated 2 Times

  • Explain Knowledge-Based Agents with the algorithm and the TELL and ASK operations.
  • Explain Wumpus World characteristics and construct/use a simple propositional knowledge base to prove the location of the Wumpus.

Also Asked

  • Apply Greedy Best-First Search to a given graph.
  • Compare Greedy Best-First Search and A* Search.
  • Explain heuristic functions and their role in informed search.
  • Explain admissible and consistent heuristics with suitable examples.
  • Prove that a consistent heuristic is admissible.
  • Construct an admissible but inconsistent heuristic.
  • Explain propositional logic, including its syntax, semantics, models and reasoning.
  • Determine whether given propositional sentences are valid, satisfiable or unsatisfiable.
  • Determine the number of models satisfying given propositional sentences.

MODULE 4 – First-Order Logic and Inference

Repeated 3 Times

  • Explain the syntax and semantics of First-Order Logic (FOL).
  • Explain Universal Instantiation and Existential Instantiation with suitable examples.

Repeated 2 Times

  • Explain the Knowledge Engineering Process and its steps in First-Order Logic.

Also Asked

  • Explain the drawbacks of propositional logic and the need for First-Order Logic.
  • Explain FOL terms, atomic sentences, universal quantifiers, existential quantifiers, nested quantifiers and equality with examples.
  • Explain Assertions and Queries in First-Order Logic.
  • Represent knowledge in the kinship domain using First-Order Logic.
  • Represent numbers, sets and lists using First-Order Logic.
  • Explain generalized modus ponens with suitable logical representations.
  • Explain the unification algorithm with suitable examples.
  • Explain FOL entailment using models.
  • Explain the difference between propositional inference and First-Order inference.
  • Represent a problem using a single FOL definite clause and suitable ground facts.

MODULE 5 – Inference, Planning and Planning Graphs

Repeated 3 Times

  • Explain the Backward Chaining algorithm with a suitable example and show the steps leading to a successful proof.

Repeated 2 Times

  • Explain Classical Planning with a suitable example such as Blocks World.
  • Explain Planning Graphs and their use for estimating heuristic values.

Also Asked

  • Explain planning as a state-space search problem.
  • Explain the two approaches to searching for a plan.
  • Explain Forward Chaining and compare it with Backward Chaining.
  • Explain how First-Order Logic sentences are converted into Conjunctive Normal Form (CNF).
  • Explain Resolution in First-Order Logic with its procedure.
  • Explain PDDL and its role in planning problems.
  • Explain the differences and similarities between problem solving and planning.
  • Prove that backward search with PDDL problems is complete.
  • Explain why dropping negative effects from action schemas produces relaxed planning problems.
  • Explain the Graphplan algorithm.
  • Write Prolog predicates and construct proof trees for suitable queries.
